*Sri Vasudeva Prabho* is a hymn to Krishna addressed as the son of Vasudeva — *vāsudeva* — and as the lord, *prabho*. The song sits in the Krishna Leela Tharangini as a tarangam of praise rather than narrative. It is set in raga Shankarabharanam and Adi tala. The Shankarabharanam setting and the song's measured tempo have made it a frequent kacheri opener and a standard piece in Carnatic concert programmes.

The full Sanskrit text — pallavi, anupallavi, and charanams praising Krishna by his many names — is in the [1913 edition on the Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/SriKrishnaLeelaTarangini). A complete transcription with translation is planned for a subsequent revision of this page.

## Meaning

The song is in the *stotra* mode: a hymn of praise, not a narrative. The composer addresses Krishna directly. The pallavi gathers four of his most familiar epithets — Vasudeva (son of Vasudeva), Prabho (lord), Jagannatha (master of the worlds), Krishna — and asks for protection. The charanams expand the list, naming Krishna for each of his major leelas: lifter of Govardhana, slayer of Kamsa, the cowherd flute-player, the husband of Rukmini.

The choice of Shankarabharanam is significant. Shankarabharanam is the 29th melakarta raga, the "ornament of Shiva", regal and complete in scale (sa ri ga ma pa dha ni — the major scale). It is used in Carnatic music for songs of cosmic praise and royal grandeur, and its presence here marks *Sri Vasudeva Prabho* as a hymn to Krishna in his fullness — not as the village child or the young cowherd but as the lord of the worlds.

## In performance

- **Carnatic concert** — the song is widely used as a kacheri opener or a closing piece. Its full Shankarabharanam scale and the natural opportunities for ragam-tanam-pallavi exposition make it a frequent choice for senior performers.
- **Bhajan tradition** — *Sri Vasudeva Prabho* is sung in southern divyanama bhajan paddhati sequences, often near the opening of a bhajan evening.
- **Bhagavata Mela** — within the Melattur and Saliyamangalam Bhagavata Mela troupes, the song appears in invocatory and concluding sequences.

## Listen

The Dr. M. Balamuralikrishna recording lists *Sri Vasudeva Prabho* in Shankarabharanam as track seven on Volume 1 of his Krishna Leela Tharangini studio set. Many other senior Carnatic musicians have recorded it; search YouTube for "Sri Vasudeva Prabho Shankarabharanam" for performances by the Bombay Sisters, Sudha Ragunathan, and others.

## Why it endures

The Tharangini is full of narrative tarangams — songs that tell episodes from Krishna's life. *Sri Vasudeva Prabho* stands a little apart from that. It is a song to be sung *at* Krishna, not *about* him. That makes it a natural piece for the opening or closing of a kacheri, when the performer wants to invoke the deity directly rather than narrate his deeds. The song's longevity in concert repertoire is partly a result of that role.
