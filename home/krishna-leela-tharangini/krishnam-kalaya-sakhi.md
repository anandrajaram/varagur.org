*Krishnam Kalaya Sakhi* is a song of yearning. A sakhi — a confidante — describes the beauty of the young Krishna to Rukmini, daughter of the king of Vidarbha, who has fallen in love with him from afar. The piece sits in the later tarangams of the [Krishna Leela Tharangini](/home/krishna-leela-tharangini/), in the run-up to the Rukmini Kalyanam. Raga Mukhari and Adi tala.

The full text — including the anupallavi and charanams that describe Krishna's flute, his peacock-feather crest, and his many forms — is in the [1913 edition of the Tharangini on the Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/SriKrishnaLeelaTarangini). A verse-by-verse transcription is planned for a later revision of this page.

## Meaning

The sakhi addresses her friend. The verb anchoring the song is *kalaya* — perceive, behold, look at. She lists what she sees: Krishna playing the flute, Krishna with the peacock feather, Krishna who is at once a child and the infinite Lord. Each verse layers another image. Rukmini, who has not seen him with her own eyes, comes to know him through the sakhi's description, and falls deeper in love.

Mukhari carries the bhava. It is a melancholic raga in the Kharaharapriya family, with a quality of longing that suits a love anticipating union but still in separation.

## In performance

- **Carnatic concert** — *Krishnam Kalaya Sakhi* in Mukhari is widely performed. It is on the Bombay Sisters' standard recital list and the Dr. M. Balamuralikrishna Krishna Leela Tharangini set.
- **Kuchipudi** — the song is regularly staged in Kuchipudi recitals, with the dancer interpreting the sakhi's description through abhinaya.
- **Bhagavata Mela** — within the Bhagavata Mela tradition of Melattur and Saliyamangalam, the song is part of the staging of the Rukmini Kalyanam episode.

## Listen

The Dr. Balamuralikrishna recording lists *Krishnam Kalaya Sakhi* in raga Mukhari as the opening track on Volume 1 of his Krishna Leela Tharangini studio set. The Bombay Sisters' version is also widely circulated. For a sense of the song in concert context, search YouTube for "Krishnam Kalaya Sakhi Mukhari" — recent renditions are uploaded by many Carnatic performers and rasikas each year.

## In the narrative

The song sits at a hinge moment in the Tharangini. In Tarangam XI, the *Rukmini Sandesa*, Rukmini sends her secret messenger — a Brahmin — to Krishna with a letter pleading him to abduct her before her wedding to Shishupala is performed. The sakhi's description of Krishna to Rukmini (this song) is part of the same emotional arc: Rukmini comes to know him through accounts before she meets him. The next tarangam closes the work with the Rukmini Kalyanam itself.
