*Alokaye Sri Balakrishnam* — sometimes catalogued as *Alokaye Rukmini* after its narrative addressee — is a song from the later tarangams of the [Krishna Leela Tharangini](/home/krishna-leela-tharangini/). The sakhi calls Rukmini to look — *ālokaya*, "behold" — at Krishna. The piece is set in raga Kambhoji and Adi tala. Kambhoji is one of the most beloved ragas in Carnatic music; the song is among the most performed of all the Tharangini tarangams.

The full Sanskrit text, including anupallavi and charanams describing Krishna's form, his ornaments, and his presence, is in the [1913 edition on the Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/SriKrishnaLeelaTarangini). A complete transcription with translation is planned for a later revision.

## Meaning

The frame is the same as *Krishnam Kalaya Sakhi*: the sakhi addresses her friend. *Sakhi* — vocative — is the form of address. The verb *ālokaya* repeats. Each charanam adds another aspect of Krishna for Rukmini to take in.

Kambhoji carries the song. An upajati raga derived from the Harikambhoji mela, it is often used for songs of cosmic praise and royal devotion. The sakhi is showing Rukmini the lord of the worlds in a young man's form, and Kambhoji is built for that.

## In performance

- **Carnatic concert** — *Alokaye Sri Balakrishnam* in Kambhoji is part of the standard concert repertoire. Dr. M. Balamuralikrishna's recording is widely circulated; many senior Carnatic musicians have recorded it.
- **Kuchipudi tarangam** — the song is among the most performed Kuchipudi tarangams. The dancer balances on the brass plate while interpreting the sakhi's call through abhinaya, with the rhythmic charanams set to footwork.
- **Music school recitals** — Kambhoji and the song's medium tempo make it a favourite for arangetrams and senior-school performance evenings.

## Listen

The Dr. M. Balamuralikrishna studio recording is the most widely cited reference. Search YouTube for "Alokaye Sri Balakrishnam Kambhoji" for both Carnatic and Kuchipudi performances; the Sridevi Nrithyalaya videos are a good starting point for the dance form.

## The narrative

Rukmini is the daughter of the king of Vidarbha. She has never seen Krishna. What she knows of him she has learned from her sakhi — songs like this one, and like *Krishnam Kalaya Sakhi*. By the time she sends her secret letter asking him to come for her on her wedding day, she has fallen for the Krishna of those descriptions. The arc closes in Tarangam XII with the Rukmini Kalyanam at Dwaraka.
