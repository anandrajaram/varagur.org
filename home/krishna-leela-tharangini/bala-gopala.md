*Bala Gopala Krishnam* is among the best-known songs from Sri Narayana Theerthar's [Krishna Leela Tharangini](/home/krishna-leela-tharangini/). It addresses the infant Krishna at Gokula, in raga Mohanam and Adi tala. The song is standard Carnatic concert repertoire and a Kuchipudi tarangam danced on the rim of a brass plate.

The full text of the tarangam — pallavi, anupallavi, and charanams — is in the [1913 edition of the Tharangini on the Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/SriKrishnaLeelaTarangini). A transcription with verse-by-verse translation will be published here in a subsequent revision.

## Meaning

The song addresses the child Krishna at Gokula. The bhava is *vatsalya* — the loving tenderness of a parent watching a young child. Sri Narayana Theerthar piles on epithets across the tarangam: cowherd, mischief-maker, Yasoda's beloved, the dark-skinned one. The refrain repeats: *pāhi pāhi* — protect me, protect me. The child's leelas become a prayer.

Raga Mohanam fits. The name itself means "the enchanter". Mohanam is pentatonic — five notes, uplifting, devotional — and matched to the auspicious image of the smiling child. It is also one of the first ragas a Carnatic student learns, which is part of why *Bala Gopala* turns up early in pedagogical repertoire.

## In performance

The song is a favourite for several reasons:

- **Carnatic concert** — *Bala Gopala* is a standard concert piece, often placed in the middle section of a kacheri as a devotional interlude. Recordings by Dr. M. Balamuralikrishna, the Bombay Sisters, and many other senior performers are widely available.
- **Kuchipudi tarangam** — in Kuchipudi recitals, *Bala Gopala* is one of the songs danced on the rim of a brass plate. The dancer balances on the raised edge of the plate while interpreting the lyrics through abhinaya and footwork, often as the climax of an arangetram.
- **Bhajan tradition** — the song is part of the divyanama sankeerthanam sequence in some southern bhajan paddhatis.

## Listen

A widely circulated reference rendition is Dr. M. Balamuralikrishna's recording of the Tharangini tarangams. The full studio set is available on YouTube and on streaming services.

For Kuchipudi performance, Sridevi Nrithyalaya and many of the Kuchipudi gurukulams have published recital videos in which *Bala Gopala* features as a tarangam piece.

## The Varagur connection

Sri Narayana Theerthar composed the Tharangini during his years at Varagur, in Thanjavur district. The tradition at the [Sri Venkatesa Perumal Kovil](/home/sri-venkatesa-perumal-kovil/) is that Lord Venkatesa danced to the tunes when the saint sang them in the sannidhi.
