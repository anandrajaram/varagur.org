*Govardhana Giridhara* praises Krishna in the moment he lifts the Govardhana hill on his little finger to shelter the cowherds, cattle, and villagers of Vraj from Indra's seven-day storm. The song sits in the sixth tarangam of the [Krishna Leela Tharangini](/home/krishna-leela-tharangini/), the *Govardhana-uddharana*. The raga most commonly cited for this song is Hindolam, but the attribution varies by pathantara.

## Pallavi

The pallavi opens with the title epithet — *Govardhana giridhara* — repeated as both invocation and praise. The full pallavi and the charanams that follow describe the cosmic significance of the leela: Indra, king of the gods, humbled by the cowherd boy; the hill borne aloft for seven days and nights; the gopas sheltered beneath. A verse-by-verse transcription will be added in a subsequent revision; the full Sanskrit text is in the [1913 edition on the Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/SriKrishnaLeelaTarangini).

## Meaning

The episode is theologically loaded. Each year the villagers of Vraj had offered rain-prayers to Indra. Krishna — still a young cowherd — persuaded them to redirect that worship to Govardhana itself, the hill that fed their cattle and watered their village. Indra was furious. A seven-day deluge came down on the village. Krishna lifted Govardhana on his little finger and held it as an umbrella; the villagers and their cattle sheltered beneath until the rains stopped. Indra came down to acknowledge him.

The bhava is *adbhuta* — wonder. The song does not narrate the leela dramatically. It praises Krishna in the moment, addressing him as the lifter of the hill and the one who humbled Indra.

## In performance

- **Carnatic concert** — *Govardhana Giridhara* is performed in concert but is less universally programmed than *Bala Gopala* or *Krishnam Kalaya Sakhi*. The setting varies by tradition; Hindolam is the most common, but recordings exist in Khamas and Kapi.
- **Kuchipudi and Bhagavata Mela** — the Govardhana lifting is one of the visually dramatic episodes in Bhagavata Mela stage productions. The tarangam is sung as accompaniment when the lifting is enacted.
- **Devotional context** — among Vaishnava bhajan groups, *Govardhana Giridhara* is sung during the autumn Govardhana Puja and Annakuta observance.

## Listen

The Dr. M. Balamuralikrishna recording of the Tharangini is the most widely circulated reference. Search YouTube for "Govardhana Giridhara Narayana Tirtha" for recent performances in the Carnatic and Kuchipudi traditions.

## The narrative

In Tarangam VI of the Tharangini, Sri Narayana Theerthar treats the Govardhana episode in detail: the false rain-worship of Indra, Krishna's redirection of the villagers' devotion, the punitive storm, the lifting of the hill, and Indra's humbling. The composer is in the Advaita tradition; the leela is treated not as a narrative episode for its own sake but as a teaching on which devotion is owed to whom.
